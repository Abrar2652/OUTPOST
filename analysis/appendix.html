The three protocol findings

These are claims about how the benchmark is measured rather than about the method, and they are the part of this work least likely to be already known to a reviewer.

Hyperparameter selection gap

What validation chose, against the best test score any swept arm reached — the advantage a paper gains by tuning on test. Every arm is scored on the same three selection seeds, the default included. On the semi-synthetic graphs validation is saturated and keeps the default. Amazon is the exception: its validation spread is 0.0090 against Photo's 0.0016, it picks a non-default arm, and that arm is 0.0040 worse on test at n=10 (nine of ten seeds, p=0.0039) than the default it displaces. Two-thirds of Amazon's 0.0105 gap is the penalty for following validation, not the reward for searching. And the discrimination is not real: recomputed over ten selection seeds instead of three, Amazon's validation margin reverses to −0.0004 and lands inside the tie band (P40). A routine three-seed procedure manufactured a preference out of noise, and following it cost measurable accuracy.

law

Detectability law

Median same-class neighbour fraction against the best achievable AUC, over 37 anomaly classes in 8 graphs. Spearman ρ = 0.7783, cluster-bootstrap 95% CI [0.3906, 0.9112], within-dataset permutation p = 5.0e-05. The permutation test is the one that matters: it shuffles inside each graph, so the correlation cannot be an artefact of easy graphs sitting above hard ones. But a correlation is not a prediction: cross-validated below, the law does not beat a constant on a graph it was not fitted on.

Does it predict a graph it was not fitted on?

Leave one dataset out, fit on the rest, predict the held-out classes. Two things have to be separated first. The feature-visible branch is definitional: the target is max(hop0…hop3) and that regime is defined by hop3 ≤ hop0, so the target equals the predictor in 13 of 16 of its classes (mean gap 0.00299). Cross-validating with that branch included makes the two-regime form look overwhelming; it is an artefact. The real test is the propagation branch against simply predicting the training mean:

noise

The tie band against the noise it thresholds

The selection rule declares two configurations different when their three-seed mean validation AUC differs by more than 0.002. That is only meaningful if 0.002 is large next to the sampling error of that comparison. It is not.

On 6 of 6 graphs the 0.002 band is smaller than the standard error of the paired comparison it decides, and on 6 of 6 it is still smaller at ten seeds. Reaching a band of one standard error would take between 11 and 51 seeds depending on the graph. So the two selections that did not survive (P35, P40) are not bad luck: on every one of these graphs the procedure resolves less than its own noise, and it fired in two of the three opportunities it was given.

The preference was noise

Same comparison, same validation split, more seeds. At the three seeds the rule uses, the selected arm leads by nearly three times the tie band. At ten it trails, well inside it. A routine selection procedure manufactured a preference out of noise, and following it cost 0.0040 AUC-ROC on nine of ten seeds (pre-registered as P40, before the ten-seed data existed).

The atlas gate does not change the count

The gate this paper ablates is a non-parametric quantile veto applied at the warmup boundary, so E400_outpost and E400_nogate record the identical parameter count on every dataset and the gate-drop result is about accuracy alone. An earlier version of this page said the full model was 0.289× DEMO and the gate was most of what OUTPOST adds; that contrast belongs to the spectral fview gate below, which is disabled in every arm the paper reports.
