## `table1_small.tex`, `table2_large.tex` — the main comparison

**Supports:** across 8 graphs at one 400-epoch protocol with identical seeds and splits, OUTPOST, the full published DEMO and NSReg from its released code land within a few points of each other, and which one leads depends on the graph.

**Does not support:** "OUTPOST is state of the art". Bold marks the best of the three same-protocol rows per column, and OUTPOST is not bold on Photo ROC, Amazon ROC or either T-Finance column. The transcribed rows above the rule come from other papers' budgets and are not comparable — that is the point of the reproduction analysis, not a ranking.

**Belongs in:** §Results, first two tables. The valsel variants belong beside them or in the appendix, never instead of them.

## `three_method_ranking.tex` — the thesis table

**Supports:** the ranking of the three methods changes between the oracle rule (per-metric max over epochs, what the published baselines used) and the deployable rule (peak-validation epoch) on half the datasets. The selection rule, not the method, decides who leads.

**Does not support:** that either rule is correct. The paper's claim is that reporting one without the other is what misleads.

**Belongs in:** §Results, main text. This is the table the contribution rests on.

## `decomposition.tex` — which component does the work

**Supports:** pseudo-labelling is the load-bearing component and the only one whose sign tracks how the benchmark was built: essential on the graphs with injected anomalies (Computers -0.0645) and worth nothing on the graphs with observed ones (Yelp +0.0059, Amazon -0.0018, T-Finance +0.0097). Conformal is not inert — it carries -0.0366 of Computers on its own — but it is nested inside the pseudo-labelling path and moves nothing on the real graphs either.

**Does not support:** "the real graphs are just harder", or "propagation cannot help on scattered anomalies". T-Finance is homophilous (same-class fraction 0.629) and pseudo-labelling still fails there, so the divider is how the labels were produced, not how the neighbourhood looks.

**Belongs in:** §5, as the mechanism result. It is also the strongest argument against a method-paper framing and should not be buried.

## `gate_off.tex` — the gate removed

**Supports:** removing the atlas gate changes nothing on any of the 7 graphs tested; the largest movement in either direction is 0.0016 (Computers), and no p-value approaches significance.

**Does not support:** that the smaller model is *better*. It is not worse, and it is 0.157x DEMO's parameters on Photo — but that ratio is unchanged by the gate, which is a non-parametric quantile veto. The gate-drop result is about accuracy only.

**Belongs in:** §5 and the efficiency discussion. Decide once whether the paper describes the gate-on or gate-off model and use that variant consistently.

## `oracle_inflation.tex` — what oracle selection is worth

**Supports:** best-over-epochs minus peak-validation is not a uniform tax. It is largest on the small semi-synthetic benchmarks (Computers +0.0795) and nearly absent on real anomaly data (Yelp +0.0023).

**Does not support:** that every published number is inflated by that much. It is the inflation *available* under the protocol those papers used, measured on our runs.

**Belongs in:** §Protocol, first.

## `hparam_selection.tex` — what tuning on test would buy

**Supports:** validation is saturated on the semi-synthetic graphs — every arm inside a 0.002 band — so a sweep there changes nothing, while choosing the best arm on test buys +0.004 to +0.017. On Amazon validation appears *not* to be saturated at the three selection seeds and picks a non-default arm that is worse on test.

**Does not support:** "validation selection is fine because it changes nothing", nor "Amazon's validation is informative". The graph that appears to discriminate at three seeds picks the arm that is worse on test, and the apparent discrimination reverses at ten seeds (P40). Saturation is not safety, and apparent signal at a small seed count is not signal.

**Belongs in:** §Protocol, beside oracle inflation.

## `baseline_selection.tex` — the same rule applied to the baseline

**Supports:** NSReg swept under OUTPOST's own validation-only rule changes configuration on 2 of 4 datasets and gets **better** on both of them. The sweep was not a courtesy extended only to our method.

**Does not support:** that the methods are now equally tuned everywhere — CS is excluded from the baseline grids for cost, and that must be said wherever a tuned number appears.

**Belongs in:** §Protocol or the appendix, cited from the fairness paragraph.

## `epoch_budget.tex`, `demo_gap.tex` — the budget decides verdicts

**Supports:** head-to-head verdicts move with training length, and most of the DEMO reproduction gap on Photo closes with nothing changed but budget.

**Does not support:** "their code does not reach their numbers". That is defensible on Computers, not on Photo, and on CS we exceed them at both budgets.

**Belongs in:** §Protocol, as the justification for the uniform 400-epoch choice — which costs us the Photo tie, and should be said so.

## `nsreg_reproduction.tex` — the published field is wrong in both directions

**Supports:** our NSReg lands above its published numbers on every dataset while our DEMO lands below its published numbers on Photo and Computers. A table mixing transcribed rows with re-run rows is not a ranking of methods.

**Does not support:** an accusation about either paper. Budget and protocol differences account for what we can attribute.

**Belongs in:** §Protocol.
